## Findings

- **High — Success threshold is too low relative to your own baseline, so conclusions can be false-positive.**  
  The plan treats `>85%` as “works well,” but your context shows simple n-gram models already hit `98–99%` on letters. That means a probe at 86–90% could still be strictly worse than shallow lexical baselines, yet be interpreted as deeper temporal representation.

```110:122:v_1/src/linear_probing/PLAN.md
→ LLM encodes temporal info beyond surface statistics. Proceed to:
...
### Outcome B: Probe works but only at early layers (accuracy ~85%, peak at layers 1-3)
→ LLM is probably just encoding surface token statistics
```
  Suggestion: redefine “strong evidence” as beating **cleaned bigram/2-5gram baselines on matched splits**, or at minimum clearly separate “above unigram” vs “competitive with strongest shallow baseline.”

- **High — Risk of selection bias (double-dipping) when choosing best layer and reporting its CV score.**  
  You CV each layer, then pick the best layer for interpretation/outcome decisions. Without a held-out test or nested CV, best-layer performance is optimistically biased.  
```83:91:v_1/src/linear_probing/PLAN.md
For each layer L:
...
3. Train ridge classifier ... using 5-fold stratified CV
4. Record: accuracy, F1 macro, per-class precision/recall
```
  Suggestion: split once into train/val/test (or nested CV), select layer/hyperparams on val, report final once on untouched test.

- **Medium — Potential confound mismatch with prior bias findings (length/surface artifacts) is not controlled in probe step.**  
  You already found strong surface separability in prior work; mean pooling + no explicit confound controls can let probe exploit token-count/length artifacts again.

  Suggestion: add explicit controls now (length-matched subsets, residualizing length/token-count, report probe with and without these controls).

## Open Questions / Assumptions

- Are you aiming to claim “LLM adds value over shallow lexical models,” or just “temporal signal exists somewhere in activations”? These need different acceptance criteria.
- Will SEAL data include reliable labels/metadata for supervised probing, or do you need weak labels / nearest-period mapping first?
- Is there grouped structure (same archive/site/scribe) that requires grouped splits to avoid leakage across folds?

## Overall take

The plan is strong operationally (clear scripts, outputs, and compute assumptions), but the **inference logic needs tightening** before implementation.  
If you fix thresholding + evaluation protocol (held-out/nested, matched baselines, confound controls), this can become a solid and defensible probing study rather than “another high-accuracy-on-easy-data” result.
