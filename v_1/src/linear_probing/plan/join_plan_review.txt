## What All 4 Reviews Agree On

These points are raised by **every single reviewer** — they form the strongest signal:

### 1. The plan is aimed at the wrong dataset
All 4 reviewers flag that the plan is built around the **4,957 letters**, while the email thread concluded the team should **pivot away from letters** (too easy, trivially separable by n-grams). The plan mentions SEAL only in passing ("Later: SEAL literary texts..."). Every reviewer says to either reframe letters as a pipeline sanity check or make SEAL the primary target.

### 2. The success threshold (>85%) is dangerously weak
The Outcome A threshold of `>85%` is barely above the unigram baseline (84.8%) and far below the n-gram baselines (96-99%). All reviewers agree this means:
- A probe hitting 86-90% could be **worse than a trivial n-gram model** but still be declared a success
- Noise from cross-validation could flip the story at that margin
- The plan risks claiming "deeper temporal representation" for what is essentially an expensive n-gram detector

### 3. Surface confounds are not controlled for
All 4 note that the data is **known** to be trivially separable by surface features (orthography, names, morphology, length). The plan identifies this risk in Step 0 (tokenization check) but then never actually controls for it in Steps 1-2. Mean pooling + no length/token-count ablations leaves the door open for the probe to exploit the same shallow signals.

### 4. Outcome A/B interpretations are overclaimed
All reviewers say that mapping "mid-layer peak" to "deeper representation" is too strong. Surface features like names, provenance markers, and orthographic conventions can survive many layers and produce a mid-layer peak without any genuine "understanding." The claims need softening.

### 5. Step 0 (tokenization check) is excellent
Universal praise. This is the right first move for extreme OOD data and should stay exactly as-is.

### 6. The plan is operationally solid
All agree the execution pipeline — scripts, storage estimates, compute assumptions, file structure — is well-structured and ready to implement. The issue is the **scientific inference**, not the engineering.

## Good Suggestions vs. Less Critical Ones

### Must-do (raised by multiple reviewers, clearly correct):
1. **Reframe letters vs. SEAL narrative** — letters = validation/calibration, SEAL = primary evaluation
2. **Raise the success bar** — define success as beating the **cleaned** baselines on **matched splits**, not just >85%
3. **Add confound controls** — length-matched subsets, token-count residualization, random-label sanity check
4. **Soften Outcome A/B/C claims** — mid-layer peak is "suggestive," not "conclusive"
5. **Align evaluation protocol** — use the same train/val/test splits as the bias check, or at least nested CV

### Should-do (unique but strong, from single reviewers):
6. **Nested CV or held-out test for layer selection** (Premium) — standard ML practice, avoids optimistic bias from picking the best of 32 layers
7. **Pin reproducibility details** (Premium) — seeds, folds, regularization grid
8. **Add EDA step before full extraction** (Gemini) — quick final-layer UMAP/PCA could save hours of GPU time if clustering is obvious or absent
9. **Check for grouped structure** (Premium) — same archive/scribe splitting across folds is data leakage

### Nice-to-have (interesting but can defer):
10. **CKA-style alignment with bag-of-n-grams** (Auto) — rigorous but may be overkill at this stage
11. **Probe-weight vs. TF-IDF correlation** (ChatGPT) — already partially covered in Outcome B interpretation

Nothing suggested across the 4 reviews is actively bad — all are constructive and relevant.

## Bottom Line: What the Plan Must Adjust

Here are the concrete changes, in priority order:

1. **Add a "Relationship to Letters vs. SEAL" subsection** — one paragraph clarifying that letters are a controlled experiment / pipeline test, and SEAL literary texts are the thesis-facing evaluation. New TF-IDF baselines must be computed for SEAL when data arrives.

2. **Redefine success criteria** — replace the `>85%` threshold with: (a) beating the **maximally-cleaned unigram baseline (69.1%)** on matched splits as minimum, (b) competing with cleaned bigram/n-gram baselines as strong evidence, and (c) using layer-peak shape + statistical significance (permutation test) rather than a single accuracy number.

3. **Fix the evaluation protocol** — either use the same 70/15/15 splits as the bias check, or use nested CV (inner loop for hyperparams, outer for evaluation). Select the best layer on validation, report final number on a held-out test set **once**.

4. **Add confound control experiments** — (a) random-label baseline to verify the probe isn't trivially fitting noise, (b) length/token-count as explicit covariates or length-matched subsets, (c) optionally correlate probe weights with TF-IDF feature importances to quantify overlap.

5. **Soften interpretation language** — Outcome A should say "suggestive of representation beyond surface statistics, pending confound controls" rather than a definitive claim. Outcome B should note that early-layer signal is *consistent with* but *not proof of* surface-only encoding.

6. **Pin reproducibility details** — add a small section specifying random seeds, fold assignment method, regularization search grid, and torch deterministic flags.

7. **Consider a quick EDA step** — extract final-layer embeddings only, run UMAP/PCA, check for obvious clustering before committing to full 32-layer extraction on GPU. This is cheap insurance.
