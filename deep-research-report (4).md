## Bottom line

If your goal is a **usable model today** for a Master’s thesis on **probing hidden states layer by layer**, my single recommendation is **`Thalesian/AKK_300m`** on Hugging Face. It is the best **publicly downloadable**, **Akkadian-specific**, **standard Transformers-compatible** checkpoint I could verify. It is explicitly fine-tuned for Akkadian cuneiform/transliteration tasks, includes a missing-sign objective, has a clear training-data description, is licensed under Apache 2.0, and can be loaded with `AutoModelForSeq2SeqLM`, which makes encoder hidden-state extraction straightforward in the standard Hugging Face workflow. citeturn36view0turn36view2turn37view0turn37view1turn39view1turn40search2

The **academically strongest probing-oriented candidate** is still the **Akkadian BERT-style masked-language-model line from Lazar et al. (EMNLP 2021)**, especially **`MBERT+Akk`**. It is the only source I found that explicitly frames itself as **the first Akkadian language model**, uses a **masked language modeling** objective on Akkadian text, and reports strong Akkadian-specific results. However, its current public discoverability is weaker than the Hugging Face checkpoints: the paper says code and trained models were released on GitHub, but I could not independently verify a clean, current Hugging Face-style checkpoint from the indexed sources I reviewed. For a thesis that needs to run right now, that availability gap matters. citeturn28view0turn30view0turn29view3

## The strongest non-ALP research candidates

The most important paper for your purposes is **Lazar et al., “Filling the Gaps in Ancient Akkadian Texts: A Masked Language Modelling Approach” (EMNLP 2021)**. The paper uses Oracc transliterations, states that Oracc contains about **10K texts, 1M words, and 2.3M signs**, and reports a preprocessed split with **8K train texts / 950K words / 1.8M signs** and **2K test texts / 250K words / 500K signs**. Architecturally, the paper evaluates three Akkadian-relevant contextual models: **`BERT+AKK(mono)`**, **`MBERT+Akk`**, and **`MBERT+Akk+Eng`**, against LSTM and zero-shot multilingual BERT baselines. The best-performing systems are the M-BERT-based ones, with overall **Hit@5 ≈ 0.89** and **MRR ≈ 0.83**, and the paper explicitly says that code and trained models were made publicly available. For probing, this is by far the most relevant literature-backed checkpoint family I found. citeturn28view0turn30view0turn29view3

The second major line is the **Gutherz, Gordin, Sáenz, Levy, and Berant** translation system from **PNAS Nexus 2023**. The user’s “Nature paper” reference is slightly off: the paper is **not in Nature**; it is **“Translating Akkadian to English with neural machine translation” in PNAS Nexus**. It uses ORACC corpora including **RINAP, RIAo, RIBo, SAAo, and Suhu**, and trains a **Fairseq convolutional NMT model**, not a transformer. The paper reports train sizes of **50,544** T2E training sentences and **45,269** C2E training sentences, plus code/data/demo availability through the **Akkademia** GitHub repo and a Colab notebook. This line is important for data and tooling, but much less ideal for hidden-state probing than the EMNLP 2021 BERT path because it is a CNN translation system rather than a contextual encoder LM. citeturn20search5turn22view0turn22view1turn23view0turn25view0turn26view0turn26view3

Outside those two, the most relevant “sanity-check” paper is **Smidt, Lefever, and de Graef (LREC-COLING 2024)**, which explicitly discusses **pre-trained embeddings**, cuneiform input choices, and fine-grained POS tagging for Old Babylonian Akkadian letters. That paper is valuable because it confirms that the Akkadian NLP community itself sees pretrained contextual representations as a live methodological issue, even if it does not introduce the public probing checkpoint you want. citeturn13search1turn14search5

Two additional cuneiform papers matter as context. **Simonjetz et al. (LREC-COLING 2024)** frame literary-text reconstruction as text matching rather than LM pretraining, and **Sahala et al. (LREC 2020)** report automated phonological transcription on about **270k training tokens**. Both are useful for understanding the broader Akkadian/RINAP/Oracc ecosystem, but neither overtakes Lazar et al. or the public Hugging Face seq2seq checkpoints for your interpretability use case. citeturn14search2turn13search0turn13search12

## The top candidates for probing Akkadian representations

### Lazar et al. MBERT+Akk

This is the most **scientifically aligned** option for probing. It is built around **masked language modeling**, uses **Akkadian transliteration** rather than translation as the core signal, and reports the best literature-backed Akkadian contextual-model results I found. Because it is an **M-BERT finetune**, it is conceptually an **encoder-only contextual model**, which is exactly what probing work usually wants. The drawback is operational, not scientific: the paper says the code and trained models are public, but I could not verify a clean, currently maintained Hugging Face distribution from the surfaced sources. citeturn28view0turn30view0turn29view3

### Thalesian/AKK_300m

This is the best **public, practical** option. It is explicitly Akkadian-focused, public on Hugging Face, Apache-2.0 licensed, and trained on a fairly substantial combination of **389,834 transliteration-only lines** plus **126,649 translated lines** from Akkademia/CDLI sources. It supports Akkadian **cuneiform**, **simple transliteration**, **grouped/complex transliteration**, **English reverse generation**, and even a **missing-sign** instruction mode. The main caveat is that it is **encoder-decoder**, not encoder-only, and it is optimized for translation/transliteration tasks rather than pure MLM-style representation learning. Still, for a thesis that needs a dependable checkpoint now, it is the cleanest choice. citeturn36view0turn36view2turn37view0turn39view1turn39view5

### Thalesian/cuneiformBase-400m

This is the best **multilingual ancient-script** option that explicitly and verifiably includes Akkadian. The card confirms training on **Akkademia, CDLI, and OARE** for Akkadian and on additional corpora for Sumerian, Hittite, Linear B, and Elamite. It is larger than AKK_300m, has a longer context window (**512 tokens**), and exposes standard HF seq2seq usage. Its Akkadian metrics are respectable, but the card also openly notes **significant domain sensitivity** on OARE out-of-domain Akkadian, and because it is multilingual, its latent space is less cleanly “Akkadian-only” than AKK_300m. For comparative work it is excellent; for a single Akkadian-focused probing target, I would rank it second. citeturn38view1turn37view3turn37view4turn37view5turn38view5

## Final recommendation

If you need **one model** for a thesis on **interpreting Akkadian representations layer by layer**, I recommend **`Thalesian/AKK_300m`**. It is the best trade-off across the criteria that matter most in practice: **confirmed Akkadian training data**, **public availability**, **clear license**, **standard Hugging Face loading**, **explicit missing-sign capability**, and **narrower Akkadian focus** than the larger multilingual alternatives. The fact that it is encoder-decoder is a real caveat, but not a fatal one: for probing, you can simply treat the **encoder stack** as your main representation source. citeturn36view0turn36view2turn37view0turn39view1turn40search2

If you are willing to tolerate harder setup and less certain checkpoint discoverability in exchange for a model that is **architecturally better aligned with classic probing methodology**, then the model family I would pursue next is **Lazar et al.’s `MBERT+Akk`** from EMNLP 2021. But because your question asks for a **single recommendation** for actual thesis work, not an idealized lab benchmark, my answer remains: **start with `Thalesian/AKK_300m`**. citeturn28view0turn30view0turn36view0turn36view2turn39view1
